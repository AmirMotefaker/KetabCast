### Research Dossier: *Leading Teams* by J. Richard Hackman

This dossier provides the foundational material for a KetabCast episode on *Leading Teams* (Persian title: *تیم ایدئال*). All content is derived from the provided official Harvard Business Review Press metadata and product description.

---

#### 1. Verified Identity & Publication Context
*   **Author:** J. Richard Hackman (a preeminent expert in group and organizational behavior).
*   **Title:** *Leading Teams: Setting the Stage for Great Performances*.
*   **Publication Date:** July 10, 2002.
*   **Publisher:** Harvard Business School Press.
*   **Core Thesis:** Team effectiveness is not the result of a specific "leadership style" or rigid formula. Instead, it is the result of a leader’s ability to create specific organizational conditions that allow a team to manage itself effectively.

#### 2. Core Concepts
*   **The "Stage-Setting" Role:** The leader’s primary job is not to micromanage daily tasks but to "set the stage" by establishing the right environment.
*   **Self-Management:** The ultimate goal of a leader is to create conditions where the team becomes capable of managing its own performance.
*   **Idiosyncratic Leadership:** There is no "one-size-fits-all" leadership style. Leaders should apply the book’s principles in ways that align with their own unique personality and the specific needs of their group.
*   **Trajectory of Effectiveness:** Success is not a static state; the goal is to launch a team onto a path where their performance improves over time.
*   **Organizational Resources:** Leaders must actively muster resources from the broader organization to support the team’s success.

#### 3. The Framework: The Five Essential Conditions
Hackman identifies five conditions that significantly increase the likelihood of team success:
1.  **A Real Team:** The group must be a bounded, stable entity with clear membership.
2.  **A Compelling Direction:** The team needs a clear, engaging purpose that motivates them.
3.  **An Enabling Structure:** The team’s internal design (tasks, composition, norms) must facilitate, rather than hinder, their work.
4.  **A Supportive Organizational Context:** The broader environment (rewards, information, resources) must provide the necessary backing for the team to function.
5.  **Available Expert Coaching:** The team must have access to guidance that helps them navigate challenges and improve their processes.

#### 4. Examples & Applications
*   **Orchestras:** Used as a model for high-performance coordination where individual expertise must align with a collective goal.
*   **Airline Cockpit Crews:** Used to illustrate how teams in high-stakes, technical environments rely on clear structures and defined roles to ensure safety and performance.
*   **Practical Application:** Leaders should audit their teams against the five conditions. If a team is struggling, the leader should ask: "Which of these five conditions is missing?" rather than blaming the team members' personalities.

#### 5. Limitations & Nuance
*   **Not a Formula:** The book explicitly warns against "subscribing to a specific formula." It emphasizes that the *application* of these principles must be adapted to the specific situation.
*   **Context Dependency:** The effectiveness of these conditions is tied to the organizational setting; a leader cannot create these conditions in a vacuum without the support of the wider organization.

#### 6. Claim Checklist for Fact QA
*   [ ] Did the leader establish a stable team membership?
*   [ ] Is the team’s purpose clear and engaging to all members?
*   [ ] Does the team have the resources (information, rewards, tools) from the organization?
*   [ ] Is the coaching focused on team processes rather than just individual tasks?
*   [ ] Is the leader applying these principles in a way that fits their own personality?
